Privacy Policy

Riftbreakers, operated by June Kingsbury (berry branch games). Effective November 18, 2026.

Riftbreakers is a single-player tactics game. It keeps your progress on your own device. Two features are optional and send data off your device: Google Drive cloud save and the online leaderboard. This policy explains what each one does with your data. If you use neither, nothing described below applies to you.

Google Drive cloud save

What Google data we access

When you connect Google Drive, Riftbreakers requests a single, highly restricted permission scope: drive.appdata. It grants access only to a hidden, application-specific folder inside your own Google Drive. Riftbreakers cannot see, read or modify any of your other Drive files, folders, documents or photos. We do not request your email address, contacts, profile details or any other Google data.

What is stored, and where

  • Save data only. The hidden folder holds a single file, riftbreak-save.json: your renown, unlocks, statistics, skill plans and any run in progress. It contains no personal information.
  • Sign-in tokens stay on your device. The tokens that keep you signed in are stored in your browser's local storage on the device you play on.
  • A small token relay. When you sign in, and when a sign-in is renewed, the request passes through a relay we run on Cloudflare Workers. The relay adds the game's OAuth client secret, so that secret never has to be published in the game, and forwards the request to Google. It does not store or log your authorization codes or tokens. Your save data never passes through it: saves travel directly between your device and your Google Drive.

How we use it

Your save data is used for one purpose only: to store your progress in your Google Drive and load it back onto your devices. We do not sell it, share it with third parties, or use it for advertising or analytics.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Riftbreakers' use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Online leaderboard

The leaderboard is public. Nothing is sent to it unless you choose to submit a winning run.

What a submission contains

  • The name you type (up to 20 characters). It is shown publicly, so please don't use your real name or anything that identifies you.
  • The run's score, the date it was won, its starting party size and Rift Tier, its battle, floor, elite and boss counts, the realms it passed through, and each party member's class and level.
  • A random run id, so the same run can't be submitted twice.

Submissions are not linked to your Google account. Submitting doesn't require signing in.

Abuse protection

To limit spam, the server keeps a one-way (salted SHA-256) hash of the IP address each submission came from, for 24 hours, and then deletes it. The address itself is not stored. Our host, Cloudflare, processes requests as described in Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Keeping and removing entries

Leaderboard entries are kept until the leaderboard is reset or the entry is removed. To have an entry removed, email us with its name, score and date, and we will delete it. We may also remove entries with offensive names or impossible scores.

On your device

Riftbreakers stores your progress, settings, the name you last submitted to the leaderboard, and cloud-sync bookkeeping in your browser's local storage. You can clear it at any time through your browser's site settings. Export your save from Settings first if you want to keep it.

Revoking access and deleting your data

  • Choose Disconnect under Settings > Save Data > Cloud Save. This signs you out and removes the stored tokens from your device.
  • Revoke the game's access entirely from your Google Account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Delete the game's hidden app data from Google Drive (Drive settings > Manage apps) to remove the cloud save permanently.
  • Email us to remove a leaderboard entry.

Children's privacy

Riftbreakers is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.
